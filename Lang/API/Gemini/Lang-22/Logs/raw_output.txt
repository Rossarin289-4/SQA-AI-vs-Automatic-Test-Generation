### Identify

1. **The behavior that differs:** 
   In the buggy version, `greatestCommonDivisor(int u, int v)` fails to handle cases where one or both of the inputs `u` and `v` are `0`. Specifically, when either `u` or `v` is `0`, the binary GCD loop enters an infinite loop or performs invalid operations because it assumes both inputs are non-zero. In the fixed version, explicit checks are added at the beginning of `greatestCommonDivisor` to handle inputs containing `0`, returning the sum of the absolute values (or throwing an `ArithmeticException` if overflow occurs with `Integer.MIN_VALUE`).

2. **The likely root cause visible from the source:** 
   The javadoc for `greatestCommonDivisor` specifies `@param u a non-zero number` and `@param v a non-zero number`, but caller methods (such as fraction reduction or arithmetic operations) can pass or result in arguments containing zero. The binary GCD algorithm relies on bit-shifting and subtraction assuming two non-zero integers; when a zero is supplied, `(u & 1) == 0 && (v & 1) == 0` behaves incorrectly, and `t` becomes zero immediately or causes division by zero / infinite looping.

3. **The specific behavior that a generated test should distinguish between the buggy and fixed versions:** 
   When reducing a fraction or calculating GCD where one of the inputs to `greatestCommonDivisor` is `0` (for instance, via `Fraction.getReducedFraction(0, 5)` or through operations that reduce fractions with a zero component), the buggy version hangs in an infinite loop or throws unexpected exceptions, whereas the fixed version correctly computes the GCD or normalizes the fraction.

4. **Boundary cases, representative cases, and regression cases that are independently designed:**
   - Reducing a fraction where the numerator is `0` and the denominator is positive or negative.
   - Calling `getReducedFraction` with zero inputs or combinations where GCD is invoked with zero.
